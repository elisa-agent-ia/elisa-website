Why company AI stalls

Your people already use AI. Mostly without you.

And everything they learn with it stays in their own chat history.

  • Personal accounts, company data. Staff paste contracts, tickets and customer data into personal AI tools. Nobody knows what left, or where it went.
  • Know-how that the company never keeps. The prompt that finally works, the right way to close the month, the fix for a recurring incident: each one stays in one person's chats. Nobody else benefits, nothing is capitalised, and it leaves with the person.
  • Assistants that chat but cannot act. A chat window cannot open the ticket or update the customer record. And an AI that acts raises hard questions (who asked, who allowed it, where the keys are), so most pilots stop at the security review. Elisa is designed to answer them.

And since February 2025, the EU AI Act requires companies that use AI to take measures to build their teams' AI literacy.

Knowledge that grows

Every conversation can make Elisa better for everyone.

When a lasting fact or a better way of doing something comes up in a conversation, or someone shares a document, Elisa offers to add it to your company knowledge, or to turn it into a new skill.

  • Proposed, then reviewed. Elisa drafts the addition and submits it for review. Nothing becomes company knowledge until a person you name approves it.

Accounting, HR, business applications, databases: Elisa works with the systems your company already runs, and you keep control of every access.

Sobriety by design

Fewer tokens by design. Fast, low-cost models are enough.

Many AI agents push your data through the model and let it do the work. Elisa does the opposite: code does the work, and the model only decides.

  • Code does the work, not the model. Skills run Python scripts in a sandbox. The data is processed there; the model chooses the skill and reads the final result.
  • A two-level index. The prompt lists only the categories a person may use; detailed skills load on demand.²
  • Compressed tool outputs. Bulky outputs (exports, logs, listings) are compressed before the model reads them, typically removing most of their size while keeping the answer.
  • Smaller models are enough. Because the model only routes and reads results, smaller, cheaper models handle most of the work.

Fewer tokens means a lower bill, and less computing, so less energy, for every request. Responsible use of AI starts with not wasting it.

  • ≈800 tokens for the always-on skill index, instead of ≈5,600 when every skill is listed² Runtime benchmark
  • 18/18 requests routed to the right skill by a fast, low-cost model³ Real-model tests

Questions

What decision makers ask first.

Who owns our skills and knowledge?

You do. They live in your company's own Git repository, and your data in your own database. At the end of the contract, your skills and knowledge are handed back to you and you can reuse them freely. If your licence ends, Elisa keeps working for 30 days, then stays readable: people can still sign in and read their data.

Does our data train AI models?

Elisa uses your data only to carry out your requests, never to train models. Prompts go to the model provider you choose, under your contract with them; with a model you host, prompts stay in your network. In an anonymized conversation, the provider reads placeholders instead of names, e-mail addresses and your clients. You choose among OpenAI, Azure OpenAI†, Anthropic†, Amazon Bedrock†, Google Vertex AI† or a model you run yourself, and decide which teams use which.

Do we need a large, expensive model?

No. Scripts do the work; the model mainly chooses the skill and reads the result, which a fast, low-cost model does well. In our tests, one routed 18 requests out of 18 to the right skill.³ You can still give some teams a larger model.

Will people actually use it?

It works where they already work: the web app, Slack† or Teams†, and on the desktop with Elisa Desktop; it replies in seven languages. You see from day one which skills are used, and how often.

We already have an office AI assistant. Why Elisa?

Office assistants write documents and summarise meetings. Elisa does the work that touches your systems (tickets, invoices, stock, reports) with the approvals and records your company needs. Many companies will use both. See what makes Elisa different →

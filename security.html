Security & trust

Built assuming the AI model will be manipulated.

Prompt injection will happen. Elisa's controls sit in the architecture, outside the model, so a manipulated model stays inside the Elisa grants of the person it works for.

Request a demo Contact us

Security is enforced by architecture, then by policy, and only last by the prompt.

At a glance

Six controls enforced outside the model.

  • Verified identity. Every request carries an identity built at sign-in, outside the model's reach.
  • Deny by default. Access starts when a role grants it. Only granted skills appear in the prompt; any other call is refused.
  • Credentials out of the prompt. A broker hands each skill call only the credentials it declares. Secrets that appear in an output are masked.
  • Isolated execution. Skill code runs in a sandbox per conversation and skill category, with no shell for the model and a network allowlist.
  • Audit trail. Every turn, tool call, approval and change, with the person's identity. Always on.
  • Your infrastructure, if you choose. On your servers with a model you host, prompts and data stay in your network.

Architecture

What stays inside, and what leaves.

In a self-hosted installation, everything runs in your network. Data leaves only on the paths below, and each one exists only if you configure it.

  1. Prompts to your model provider, if you use a cloud model. With a model you host, they stay in your network.
  2. Web pages read from their address, only for roles granted web access.
  3. Skill traffic to the hosts and ports each skill declares.
  4. Calls to Google or Microsoft† for a person's own mail and calendar (and Google Drive files), after they link their account and approve.
  5. Audio for transcription, to the hosted provider you configure, if you enable it.
  6. Notifications by e-mail, Slack† or webhook, and messages in Slack† or Teams† if you connect them.
  7. Your identity provider and your Git host, if they run outside your network.
  8. Audit events and telemetry to the SIEM† or collector† you configure.
  9. Image downloads from your Elisa registry (or your mirror) during installs and upgrades.

Fully independent. The running software works on its own: the licence is checked offline, and Elisa never contacts us. Evaluation (free for up to 5 people, 30 days) and a licensed deployment are enforced the same way, wherever you run it.

Encryption at rest

Encrypted at rest, by default.

Elisa encrypts conversations and files itself, before they reach the database, the disk or the bucket. It is on from installation, with nothing to configure: a copy of your data without the key is unreadable.

Conversations

  • Messages, tool arguments and results, titles, summaries and memory
  • Approvals, scheduled prompts, run checkpoints, sign-in tokens and file names; tags through a keyed index
  • AES-256-GCM, value by value; search still works: Elisa decrypts and scans inside

Files

  • Workspaces, uploads, previews and object storage, under opaque names
  • AES-256-GCM in segments, with keys per conversation: deleting a conversation shreds the keys of its files
  • Sandboxes work on a copy in memory, synced around each command; no host folder is mounted into them

Keys and backups

  • One store key, separate from the vault key; self-hosted, it stays on your servers. On an instance we host, we keep it encrypted, with an offline copy; it is handed to you on request and at the end of the contract
  • Backups hold ciphertext and the ids of the keys, never a key
  • Built-in key rotation; keep an offline copy of the store key, like any master key

What it protects. Every copy of your data: a database dump, a backup, a disk or volume snapshot, a bucket, a stolen disk, or tables read outside Elisa.

Model providers

You choose who sees your prompts.

  • Elisa uses your data only to carry out requests, never to train models.
  • With a cloud model, the provider sees the prompts sent to it, under your contract with them. Elisa can reach Bedrock† and Vertex AI† through private endpoints in your cloud network.
  • With a model you host (any OpenAI-compatible server), prompts stay in your network.
  • Roles decide which models each person may use, so a team handling sensitive data can be kept on a model you host.
  • With any model, anonymized conversations send placeholders instead of names, e-mail addresses and your clients (see below).

Anonymized conversations

The model works on placeholders. You keep the real data.

In an anonymized conversation, Elisa rewrites every request inside your perimeter before it leaves for the AI model, then puts the values back in the answer. Nothing to change in your skills or your habits.

What is replaced

  • Names and e-mail addresses of your users
  • E-mail addresses, phones, IBANs, card numbers, IP addresses
  • Personal and business fields of database and API results: names, e-mails, companies, addresses
  • People's names in mails and documents
  • Your clients, sites and projects, from a list you review

How the values come back

  • The same value keeps the same placeholder for the whole conversation
  • Answers are restored as they stream; tool arguments before the tool runs
  • Summaries and titles are made from placeholders and stored with the values
  • Web page addresses receive the placeholders, never the values
  • Images are read locally as text, then anonymized

Who decides

  • Each person, one click per conversation, with a view of exactly what the model receives
  • Your organisation: on by default, always on for chosen roles (HR, finance, legal)
  • Models you run yourself read the real values; a local model can also spot names in long texts
  • Audit records how many values were replaced, by kind, never the values

Controls

The controls, domain by domain.

Identity & access: company sign-in, roles from your groups, stale access stops.

OpenID Connect sign-in with PKCE and back-channel logout · several identity providers at once, never linked to one another · roles recomputed on every request · tokens and chat accounts stop after 30 days without a sign-in (configurable) · local passwords refused in production unless explicitly allowed · personal API tokens stored hashed, optionally narrowed to chosen skills · rate limits per person, and per IP on sign-in and failed authentication · an audited emergency-admin switch, set from the environment.

Authorisation & approvals: deny by default, approvals by kind of action.

Deny by default · company action classes, unknown ones refused · approvals by requester, data owner or named role · four eyes unless a rule allows self-approval · approvers see masked arguments and approve exactly those · usage limits per class · sub-agents can only narrow permissions.

Data protection: private by default, confidential mailbox data, anonymized conversations.

Private conversations by default; another person's conversation answers as “not found” · share links store only a hash, expire after 90 days by default, can be revoked and still need sign-in · personal memory readable by its owner only, with no admin view, and erasable · a conversation that reads a person's mailbox, calendar or Drive becomes confidential: all shares removed, new shares refused, web reads ask first · anonymized conversations: the model reads placeholders instead of names, e-mails, numbers and your terms, per conversation or enforced per role · deleting a conversation deletes its files at once and shreds their keys · Elisa Desktop reads files on a person's computer only when your instance turns it on and a role grants device-read, only in the folders that person allows, and treats them as external content: writes that follow ask for confirmation.

Credentials: a vault apart, credentials out of the prompt.

Vault in its own database, each value encrypted with AES-256-GCM and bound to its record · master key kept apart from database credentials and backups, optional wrapping by your KMS† · credentials placed in the environment of the skill call that declares them, never in the prompt, then added to redaction · web addresses containing a recognised secret are blocked.

Execution isolation: sandbox per category, network denied by default.

Sandbox per conversation and skill category (categories share the conversation's workspace files) · read-only root file system, skill code mounted read-only · no shell for the model: it can only run reviewed skill scripts · processes ended after each command · the sandbox works on a private copy of the conversation's files in memory, synced before and after each command, and released when idle; no host folder is mounted for it, and the copy back ignores symbolic links and special files · network denied by default, allowed per skill and port through a proxy · no route to package indexes, private and cloud-metadata addresses refused, unless a skill declares such a host or you allow a private range · HTML previews isolated with no network.

Prompt-injection containment: untrusted content is data, never authority.

Web content wrapped and marked as data · writes after reading external data need the requester's confirmation · that confirmation never replaces a named approver: the requester cannot approve their own payment, whatever the model says · an injected instruction can at most propose a change to skills or knowledge, which a human must review and merge · web fetch guarded against internal addresses, redirects rechecked · the web app never loads images in model answers.

Logging & audit: always on, exportable.

Append-only audit in the application, stored in the database, always on; purged only by your retention policy · people see their own trail; auditors see everyone's and export CSV or JSONL (up to 100,000 rows), and the export is audited too · SIEM export by signed webhook, syslog over TLS or OpenTelemetry† · metrics and traces† without message content.

Change control: reviewed changes, invalid ones never load.

Skills, knowledge and policies change only through your governance repository; with branch protection, only by reviewed merge request · automatic checks for secrets, manifests and network rules · protected branches and optional signed commits, configured on your Git host · invalid commits are held back.

Secure configuration & supply chain: strict production settings, traceable images.

Secrets read from files, never logged · unknown settings rejected in production · development shortcuts refused in production · no plugin code downloaded at run time · images published with build provenance and an SBOM · third-party licences listed.

Vulnerability management: dated fixes, announced.

Critical fixes released within 7 days of confirmation and announced to customers by e-mail to your named contacts, and the release notes · every image published with its build provenance and SBOM, which our customers can verify.

Encryption: TLS in transit; conversations, files and vault encrypted at rest.

TLS in transit, terminated by your reverse proxy or ingress · conversation content sealed in the database with AES-256-GCM under a store key of its own: messages, tool arguments and results, titles, tags (through a keyed index), summaries, memory, approvals, scheduled prompts, run checkpoints, sign-in ID tokens and file names · workspaces, uploads, previews and object storage encrypted in segments with AES-256-GCM, keys per conversation, opaque names · on by default; production refuses to start without the store key, or with a key that did not encrypt the data · key rotation built in; backups hold ciphertext and key ids, never a key · the vault, and the mailbox-access tokens it stores, encrypted with its own master key when you use the built-in vault (by your provider with an external one) · disk encryption underneath adds defence in depth.

What we have tested, and what comes next.

  1. Done · Internal red team (Nov 2026) Scope: a curious colleague, secret exfiltration, privilege escalation through the agent. All high and medium findings fixed¹.
  2. Done · Internal red team on encryption at rest (Nov 2026) Scope: someone holding a copy of the database, disk, bucket or backups; code in a sandbox; an operator mistake. All high and medium findings fixed, with regression tests¹.
  • Internal red-team review (10 November 2026). Method: a manual review by the team, assisted by AI tools. The 18 proof-of-concept attacks are kept as automated tests that run in CI on every pull request.
  • Example finding (high). A symbolic link planted in a conversation's workspace could become a read-write mount of a host folder in a sub-agent's sandbox. Fixed: Elisa now refuses symbolic links when it reads, writes or mounts workspace files; the attack is an automated test.
  • Red-team pass on encryption at rest (15 November 2026). Fixed in the same pass; each proof of concept is kept as a regression test. Example (high): the documented key rotation re-encrypted the database but left the files' keys under the old key, so removing it would have lost every file. Now one command rotates both, and Elisa refuses to start while any file key needs a key it does not hold.
  • Upgrade safety: install, upgrade and failed-upgrade rollback are tested automatically on every pull request.

Security FAQ

Is each installation dedicated to one company?

Yes. Each company gets its own installation and database, whether on your servers or ours.

Who sees a person's conversations?

By default, only that person. They can share with a colleague, with a team they belong to, or with a read-only link that still requires sign-in. Approvers see the masked arguments of the action they decide.

Can administrators read conversations?

Conversations stay private to their owner: the application gives administrators no view of another person's messages. Holders of the audit role see the audit trail, which records actions, not message content. In the database, conversation content is encrypted. Manage the store key and the running Elisa servers as privileged access, like any production secret.

Can someone with database or backup access read our conversations?

Only with the store key. Messages, titles, summaries, memory and the rest of the conversation content are ciphertext in the database, its dumps and its backups, and files are ciphertext on the disk or in the bucket. The audit trail records actions, never message content.

How do we investigate an incident?

The audit trail shows who did what, with which skill and which approval. Conversations are kept according to your retention policy; their owner can share them with the people investigating.

Is data encrypted?

Yes. In transit by TLS. At rest, by default: Elisa encrypts conversation content and files with AES-256-GCM under a store key kept apart from the vault key, and the vault with its own master key. Disk encryption underneath adds defence in depth. What is encrypted

How do backups work?

elisactl backup saves the database, the vault database and the encrypted conversation files (uploads too, with local file storage), and every upgrade backs up first. Archives hold ciphertext and the ids of the keys in use, never a key. Keep an offline copy of the store key apart from the backups, like any master key. On Kubernetes, use your PostgreSQL and volume backups.

How is data deleted?

By the retention rules in your repository, every night, for what they list. People can erase their personal memory. Deleting a conversation deletes its files at once and shreds their keys; backups follow your backup rotation.

Do you have access to our instance?

Self-hosted: only if you grant it, for support. Hosted by us: only named staff access it, for operations, upgrades and incidents. No Elisa screen shows them your conversations, which are encrypted in the database.

Where is a hosted instance?

In France, with OVHcloud, a French hosting provider, when you choose hosting and management by us: a dedicated instance and database for your company.

Who are your subprocessors?

Self-hosted: none. Your data stays on your servers, and the model provider you choose works under your own contract. Hosted by us: OVHcloud, for hosting, in France. Only if you entrust them to us, add the AI model's provider when we provide model access, and the host of your governance repository when we host it. The current list is in the data processing agreement.

Does Elisa call home?

Never. The running software works on its own: the licence is a signed file checked offline, with no licence server, telemetry or update check. Installs and upgrades download images from the registry you configure. Evaluation (free for up to 5 people, 30 days) and a licensed deployment are both enforced the same way, wherever you run it.

How is the source protected?

The modules that carry the most value (licensing and governance logic) ship compiled, not as plain Python, so a copy of the image does not hand over the source. Everything else — plugins, the SDK, skills — stays readable, so you can review it and a partner can build against it.

Report a vulnerability

Found a problem? Tell us first.

Write to us with what you found and how to reproduce it. We acknowledge within 2 business days and keep you informed until it is fixed.

Scope. The Elisa software, tested on your own installation (an evaluation install is free for up to 5 people), and the elisa-agent.com website. Our customers' instances are out of scope: do not access them without their consent.

What we ask of you.

  • Tell us as soon as possible.
  • Avoid privacy violations, service disruption, and destroying or changing data.
  • Exploit a flaw only as far as needed to show it exists: no data extraction, no persistent access, no pivoting to other systems.
  • Give us reasonable time to fix it before any publication.

Our commitment. Good-faith research that follows this policy is welcome. We will not bring or support legal action for accidental breaches of this policy. If a third party takes action against you while you followed it, we will make it known that you acted within it.

How it works · Governed like code

From a question to an audited action.

Elisa is a small core with pluggable edges. Your identity provider, models, Git host, sandbox and secret store plug in; the security path in the middle is always the same, for skills and for connectors alike.

One request

What happens when Alice asks.

Alice works in finance. She asks: “Pay invoice INV-2291 to Larkspur Supplies (€18,400).”

  1. Resolve. Alice signed in through your identity provider. Elisa builds her identity (company, person, verified e-mail, groups, grants) once, at the gateway, outside the reach of the model and the plugins.
  2. Authorize. Her roles must grant the payment skill and every action class it declares, here payment, a class your company defines. Only granted skills appear in her prompt; any other call is refused.
  3. Policy. Your approval rule says a payment needs a member of finance-leads other than Alice herself. The run pauses. Jordan approves in his Approvals inbox; his decision covers exactly the arguments he saw. The run resumes, even hours later, even after a restart, within the rule's timeout.
  4. Untrusted content. Had the conversation read external content (a web page, an attachment, an untrusted connector's output, someone's mailbox), Elisa would ask Alice to confirm before any write. Here, the conversation holds trusted content only.
  5. Credentials. The credential broker puts the bank credential that the skill declares into that call's sandbox, and nowhere in the prompt.
  6. Execute. The model runs reviewed skill scripts only, never a shell. This one runs in a sandbox for this conversation and skill category, and reaches only api.bank.example:443, the host the skill declares. It works on a copy of the conversation's files held in memory; on disk, they stay encrypted.
  7. Redact. Secrets, the credentials in use and your company's patterns (here, the bank-details pattern you defined) are masked in the tool output and in the reply, while it streams.
  8. Audit. Each step is recorded with Alice's and Jordan's identities.

Skills

Your processes, written down once.

A skill is a folder: instructions in a SKILL.md file, optional scripts, and a manifest the model never sees.

  • The manifest is the contract. It declares the skill's action classes, the credentials it may receive, the hosts and ports it may reach, and whether it reads a person's own data. Elisa enforces it on every call.
  • Writes can require a confirmation flag. Without the flag, the call runs as a preview (a convention your reviewers check) and needs no approval; with it, the write classes and their approval rules apply.
  • Organised by category. Each category has a router skill and specialised skills, plus shared code and knowledge notes. Company-wide helper code lives in one shared folder, without credentials.
  • Small prompts, by design. A person's prompt lists only the categories they are granted; detailed skills load when needed. In our benchmark, the always-on index of a 100-skill catalogue was about 800 tokens instead of about 5,600¹.
  • A file format you may already use. Skills use the SKILL.md format (a name and a description at the top) that several AI agents read, plus an elisa: manifest. Moving skills from a personal agent is mostly renames, a manifest and small script changes; we provide a migration guide and tooling.
  • Ready-made packs. PDF reports and letters in your house style (colour, logo, name, footer), spreadsheets, charts, OCR, transcription (through the provider you configure), Google mail, calendar and Drive, Microsoft† mail and calendar, Slack†, knowledge tools, and Elisa's own help and proposal skills.
---
name: acme-support-incidents-open
description: Open an incident in the ticketing system. NOT for reading tickets.
elisa:
  actions: [prod-write]
  credentials: [tickets_api]
  network: ["tickets.acme.internal:443"]
  runtime: python3
  confirm_flag: --yes
---

Governance

Every change goes through a merge request.

Each company has one Git repository on its own GitLab or GitHub (or a local repository to start). It holds every skill, specialist agent, knowledge note and policy. Turn on branch protection, and nothing goes live unreviewed.

  • What is in it: skills/, agents/, knowledge/, and policies/ for roles, action classes, approvals, redaction and retention.
  • Kept elsewhere: secrets, model keys and identity-provider settings stay out of it; a secret scan blocks accidental commits. Role files map e-mails or group names: keep the repository internal, like any company configuration.
  • How a change goes live: a merge request, an automatic check in your CI (elisa repo lint), a reviewer's approval under your Git host's rules, a merge. Elisa loads the head of the tracked branch within minutes (webhook, or polling every 5 minutes by default), validates it and reloads it without a restart. A commit that fails validation is held back while the previous version stays live.
  • The checks: manifests, network declarations and secrets committed by mistake block the merge; overlapping skill descriptions and orphan notes raise warnings.
  • Git is the single source. Roles and policies come from the repository alone, apart from an audited emergency-admin switch. Who holds a role follows your identity provider's groups.
  • Elisa proposes, people decide. Ask Elisa to add a skill or fix a note: it drafts the change, runs the checks and opens the merge request on a dedicated branch, attributed to you. Only platform admins may propose policy changes.

Permissions

Who may do what, written in one file.

Roles map your identity provider's groups to what people may use. Deny by default: access starts when a role grants it.

  • Roles grant skills, action classes, knowledge categories, models and a budget.
  • Action classes describe what a call does: read, write, prod-write, delegated-data, web, plus classes you define, such as payment or customer-send. An unknown class is refused.
  • Approval rules name who approves each class: the person asking, the data owner, or members of a named role. Four eyes by default: self-approval only where a rule explicitly allows it.
  • Approvers see what they approve: the arguments, with secrets masked, and the approval covers exactly those arguments.
  • Usage limits cap a class per person or for the whole company, for example 50 payments a day.
  • Approvals wait as long as the rule allows. With no timeout, a run waits indefinitely; approvals deep in a multi-agent run pause and resume the whole run.
  • One inbox. Approvals arrive in the web app, with a notification by e-mail, Slack† or webhook. Slack Approve/Reject buttons are optional.
rules:
  - action: payment
    approver: named
    roles: [finance-leads]
    timeout: 4h
    reason: Money leaves the company.

Knowledge

What your company knows, reviewed and linked.

Knowledge notes are Markdown files in your repository: facts, systems, projects, incidents, decisions, runbooks and who does what.

  • Notes link to each other and carry typed properties. You can add your own types.
  • Changes go through merge requests and are checked for broken links, orphan notes and committed secrets.
  • Each role sees only the categories it is granted; other notes stay invisible to it.
  • The Knowledge page shows search, the link graph, a health view (orphans, broken links, stale notes), each note's Git history and a “Propose a change” button.

Curated by design. This knowledge base holds what your company has decided is true.

Channels and automation

In the web app, in chat, by voice, through the API.

Web app

Conversations with a file workspace and previews of PDFs, tables, images and code; Office files preview as text (faithful rendering with an optional add-on). Approvals inbox, usage and activity log. Interface in English and French.

Slack† and Microsoft Teams†

Mention Elisa in a channel. Chat accounts are matched to people who already signed in to the web app.

API

A versioned public API with streaming, and personal API tokens you can narrow to chosen skills and action classes.

Replies in the person's language

English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese or Dutch.

Sub-agents and specialist agents

Sub-agents work in parallel, with permissions narrowed from the parent's. Specialist agents (a role prompt, allowed skills, a model) are defined in your repository and reviewed like skills.

Scheduled jobs

Run for a person or a team with the owner's current permissions; runs stop automatically when the owner leaves or loses access.

Connectors (MCP)†

Import an MCP server reachable over HTTP as a reviewed skill category, through a merge request, with approval rules for its writing tools.

Usage and budgets

Know what every answer costs.

  • Every model call is recorded; it is priced as soon as you enter your model prices.
  • Budgets per role, monthly in US dollars or daily in tokens, checked before each model call. A warning at 80 % by default.
  • Each person sees their own usage; platform admins see the whole company's.
  • Prompts stay small: permission-filtered skill lists, prompt-cache friendly ordering, compression of large tool outputs, and concise reply styles. Admins see the tokens saved by compression, per skill.

Your business tools inside Elisa

Your CRM and back offices, one click from the assistant.

Your teams open their business tools from Elisa's sidebar, at your Elisa address, with the same sign-in and the same roles. Elisa works in the same tools through its skills.

  • One address, one sign-in. A CRM at /crm, reports at /apps/reports: people stay signed in with their company account.
  • Roles decide who sees each tool. Sales see the CRM, finance sees the reports; nobody else.
  • Your own tools, or a vendor's. A web app you built, or a tool with its own server: Elisa serves it and can tell it who the person is, signed, so there is no second login.
  • Secure by default. Each tool gets its own security policy, cannot be embedded elsewhere, and every opening is recorded.
  • Installed in minutes. One command per tool, and Elisa's updates never touch them.
./elisactl app add crm --npm @acme/crm@2.4.0 \
    --dir dist/admin --path /crm --title CRM

# policies/roles.yaml
roles:
  sales:
    apps: [crm]

Elisa Desktop

Type or ask out loud. Elisa answers out loud, and on screen.

Elisa Desktop, the companion app for macOS and Raspberry Pi, connects to your Elisa with the person's own account. Chat by text or speak out loud; the same skills, roles and approvals apply as in the web app.

  • An answer to hear, the details to read. Speak and Elisa answers in one or two spoken sentences, then shows the details on screen. The conversation reads normally in the web app afterwards.
  • In the language spoken. English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese or Dutch, message by message.
  • Approve by voice. Approvals waiting for the person are decided from Elisa Desktop, with a dedicated token created in one click on the Tokens page.
  • Your files, with your consent. “Find the lease I signed in March”: Elisa searches, lists and reads files on the computer, only in the folders allowed in Elisa Desktop, and only for messages sent from that computer.
  • Governed like everything else. Off until you turn it on; then roles decide who may use it (the device-read action class), and every call shows in the conversation's activity, stored encrypted.

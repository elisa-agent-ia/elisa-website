What makes Elisa different

Built around your business skills, governed by your rules.

Elisa is an AI agent orchestrator that does real work on your systems. What sets it apart is how it works: your know-how becomes company skills, every change is reviewed, security sits outside the model, and you choose where it runs and who runs it.

Request a demo See how it works

01 · Your business skills

Your know-how is the core, and it belongs to you.

Each skill encodes how your company does a task: instructions, and the scripts that do the work on your systems. Instead of prompts scattered across personal accounts, you get one library of skills, shared by the teams you authorise.

  • One library for the whole company. Support, finance and IT use the same reviewed skills; you decide which team may use which.
  • Owned by your company. Skills and knowledge live in your own Git repository, not in someone's chat history.
  • Handed back at the end of the contract. You keep them and can reuse them freely.

How skills work

02 · Knowledge that grows

Every conversation can make Elisa better for everyone.

When a lasting fact or a better way of doing something comes up in a conversation or a shared document, Elisa offers to add it to your company knowledge, or to turn it into a new skill. Once a person you name approves it, every team granted that knowledge benefits.

  • Proposed, then reviewed. Nothing becomes company knowledge without a human approval.
  • Linked and searchable. Notes on your systems, projects, decisions and runbooks, linked to each other and to the skills that use them.
  • Seen only by the roles granted it. Each role sees its own knowledge categories.

How knowledge works

03 · Governed like code

Every change is a reviewed merge request, in your own Git.

Skills, knowledge, roles and approval rules live in one repository on your GitLab or GitHub. A change goes live after automatic checks and a reviewer's approval, under your own branch protection, and Elisa reloads it without a restart.

  • Elisa proposes, people decide. Elisa drafts improvements itself, runs the checks and opens the merge request; your reviewers approve.
  • The change record auditors ask for. Every change has its author, reviewer and date.
  • Invalid changes never load. A commit that fails validation is held back while the previous version stays live.

How governance works

04 · Acts on your systems, safely

It does the work on your tools, within limits you set.

Elisa opens the ticket, updates the record and builds the report. Each skill declares what it needs, and Elisa enforces that declaration on every call.

  • Declared systems only. Each skill lists the hosts and ports it may reach; everything else is refused.
  • Credentials stay out of the prompt. They come from your vault for that call only, never through the conversation.
  • Deny by default. Nothing is allowed until a role grants it.
  • Approvals by kind of action and role. Payments, production changes and customer e-mails wait for the right approver. Nobody approves their own request.

How approvals work

05 · Security outside the model

Built assuming the model will be manipulated.

Controls sit around every tool call, not in the prompt. A tricked model cannot go beyond what the person's roles allow, and anything sensitive waits for a human.

  • Identity Verified on every request, from your company sign-in.
  • Grants Checked for every skill and every kind of action.
  • Approvals Applied by rule, covering exactly the arguments the approver saw.
  • Isolation Reviewed scripts run in a sandbox; the model never gets a shell.
  • Masking Secrets and sensitive patterns masked in results and replies.
  • Anonymized conversations The model reads placeholders instead of names, e-mail addresses, numbers and your clients; Elisa puts the values back in the answer.
  • Encryption at rest, by default Conversations and files, AES-256-GCM, with a key that stays on your servers when you host Elisa.

Every step is recorded. Read the security model →

06 · Your model, your infrastructure

Your servers or ours. Your model, always.

Use the model provider you choose, or a model you host on any OpenAI-compatible server, so prompts stay in your network. Run Elisa on your own servers, or on a dedicated instance we host for you in the EU. Roles decide which teams use which model. With a hosted model, anonymized conversations keep names, e-mail addresses and your clients out of the prompts (how).

The same software everywhere: only who operates it changes. Deployment and models →

07 · Code does the work, the model decides

Fewer tokens by design. Fast, low-cost models are enough.

Rather than pushing your data through the model, Elisa runs the skill's scripts in an isolated sandbox. The data is processed there; the model chooses the skill and reads the result.

  • A two-level skill index. The prompt lists only the categories a person may use; detailed skills load on demand.¹
  • Compressed tool outputs. Bulky exports, logs and listings are compressed before the model reads them.
  • A lower bill, less energy. Smaller, cheaper models handle most of the work: a responsible, sustainable use of AI.
  • ≈800 tokens for the always-on skill index, instead of ≈5,600 when every skill is listed¹ Runtime benchmark
  • 18/18 requests routed to the right skill by a fast, low-cost model² Real-model tests

08 · Full audit, controlled costs

Know who did what, and what every answer costs.

Every action is recorded with who asked and who approved, in your own infrastructure. Every model call is recorded too, so you know what each team spends.

  • An audit trail that is always on. Auditors see everyone's trail and export it.
  • Budgets per role, checked before each model call, with a warning before the limit.
  • Usage you can read. Each person sees their own usage; admins see the whole company's, including the tokens saved per skill.

Usage and budgets

09 · You run what you want

Run it yourself, or let us run it, part by part.

For each part, you choose: your team, or ours. A dedicated project manager gathers your processes and tools and turns them into skills; you review and approve each one before it goes live.

Servers

Your own servers, or a dedicated instance hosted, monitored and backed up by us in the EU.

Model

The provider you choose or a model you host, or model access provided by us.

Operations

One command per operation for your team, or upgrades, backups and key rotation handled by us.

Skills

Written by your teams, or written and kept up to date by us.
